# Inbox Radar

School inboxes mix professor emails, TA requests, advisor follow-ups, club newsletters and a hundred automated
notices. The emails that need an answer sink, and the asks inside them ("send me your topic by Friday") never make
it onto any list. Inbox Radar reads the inbox once a day, drafts the replies, and keeps one to-do list current.

## When to use

- "check my email" / "what emails do I need to answer?"
- "help me reply to Prof. Lee"
- "make me a to-do list"
- "I'm drowning in email"
- The daily check-in below and Sunday Radar's brief

## Steps

### 1. Sort the inbox

If Google isn't connected, ask the student to connect it and stop. Read the student's email from the last 3 days
(or since the last run), and sort each message into one of these:

- **Needs a reply**: a person (professor, TA, advisor, classmate, recruiter, club officer) asked the student a
  question or for something, and the student hasn't replied in the thread.
- **Has a task**: something to do with or without a reply ("submit the form by Friday", "bring your laptop to lab",
  "pay the deposit", "sign up for a demo slot").
- **Has a date**: exams, meetings, events or deadlines.
- **FYI or noise**: newsletters, automated notices, promotions. Leave these out unless the student asks.

Never reply to, archive, delete or label anything unless the student asks.

### 2. Draft replies

For each email that needs a reply, write a short draft in the student's voice: answer what was asked, confirm dates,
and ask any question that's needed. For professors and TAs, follow Cubbie's usual way of drafting professor emails.
If a reply depends on something only the student knows (a choice, a date, an attachment), ask them that one thing
first.

Save each draft as a Gmail draft in the same thread, so the student can review and send from their phone. Never send.
If the student says "send it" for a specific draft, show it once more and send only after a clear yes.

### 3. Build the to-do list

Turn every task and deadline into a Google Tasks item in a list called "Campus Radar", with the due date, a short
title that starts with a verb ("Send project topic to Prof. Lee"), and the email's subject in the notes. Skip items
already on the list, and mark items done when the student says they've done them.

- Emails about an exam: hand the date to Exam Radar when it's installed, so countdowns and practice questions start.
- Emails about club events: hand them to Club Radar when it's installed.
- Meetings with a set time: offer to add them to Google Calendar, and add them only after a yes.

### 4. Text the summary

One short text: how many replies are drafted, the most urgent one or two, and the top three to-dos.

```text
3 replies drafted in Gmail:
- Prof. Lee asked for your project topic by Fri (most urgent)
- Advisor wants a meeting time
To-do:
- Send project topic (Fri)
- Sign up for sprint demo slot (Thu)
- Pay robotics club dues (Sun)
```

### 5. Daily check-in

Offer a daily run once (default 8 am), worded so it stands alone: "Inbox Radar daily: sort the student's email since
yesterday, save Gmail drafts for anything needing a reply, update the Campus Radar to-do list, and text a short
summary." Create it only after a yes. On days with nothing new that needs action, don't text.

## Replies

- Short, plain text, no Markdown. Lead with what's urgent.
- Never paste whole emails into a text; one line per email.
- Drafts are polite, short and specific. No made-up facts, dates or commitments.

## When something fails

- Google not connected: ask the student to connect it and stop.
- A draft can't be saved: send the draft as a text instead and say so.
- Inbox clean: "Nothing needs a reply today." Only when the student asked.

## Outside Cubbie

Without Gmail, ask the student to paste the emails, and give the drafts and the to-do list in the reply.
