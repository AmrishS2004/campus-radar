# Catch Me Up

Missing a few days of class is easy. Catching up is the hard part: new slides in three courses, a due date that
moved in an announcement, a discussion thread where the TA said what's on the exam, and an email from a professor
asking where you were. Catch Me Up collects all of it into one document and drafts the emails the student needs to
send.

## When to use

- "emergency, I'm in the hospital" (emergency mode, below)
- "I was sick since Tuesday, what did I miss?"
- "I'm back from a conference, catch me up"
- "I missed CSE 310 on Thursday"
- "I'm so behind, help"

## Emergency mode

When the student says something urgent is happening right now (hospital, family emergency, accident, a death, can't
make it to an exam today), speed matters more than completeness. Do this first, and do the full catch-up later.

1. If they might be in danger, tell them to contact emergency services (911 in the US) first. Don't ask for
   details about what happened beyond what they offer.
2. From Canvas, find every course with an exam, quiz, deadline, presentation or in-person requirement in the next
   7 days, plus courses meeting in the next 48 hours.
3. Draft one short email per affected professor, all at once: says there's an emergency (only the detail the student
   wants shared), names what's affected and when, asks how to make it up, and says documentation can follow if the
   syllabus asks for it. Address each to the professor (and the TA if the syllabus says to), from the course's
   syllabus or Canvas contact info. Use Cubbie's usual way of drafting professor emails.
4. Text one message listing who will get an email and what it says in one line each, then ask: "Send all 3?" The
   student can say "send all", pick some, or change one. Send only what they approve, and confirm which went out.
5. Offer a reminder for tomorrow: "Catch Me Up: check for professor replies about the emergency and start the
   catch-up PDF." Then, when the student is ready, run the normal steps below.

Never send anything in emergency mode without the student's yes, even if they seem rushed. One "send all" is enough.

## Steps

### 1. Pin down the window

Work out the start and end of the absence from what the student said ("since Tuesday" means Tuesday morning until
now). If it's unclear, ask one question with a default: "Since Tuesday morning?" If they missed only one course,
cover only that course.

### 2. Collect what they missed

If Canvas isn't connected, ask the student to connect it and stop. For each current course, within the window:

- New or updated lecture slides, files, pages and modules (what the lectures covered, from the slide titles).
- Announcements, especially changed dates, room changes, exam info and "make sure you" instructions.
- Assignments: new ones, ones due during the absence (submitted or missing), ones due in the next 7 days, and any
  date changes. Note which ones are now late.
- Discussion posts from the instructor or TAs, especially about exams or assignments.
- Quizzes or in-class activities that happened during the absence, if Canvas shows them.

From Gmail and synced email, collect messages from professors, TAs and course staff in the window, and any that ask
for a reply.

If Slide Radar is installed, also include its finds: tasks mentioned only in the slides from the missed lectures.

### 3. Make the catch-up PDF

One PDF titled "Catch-up: <start> to <end>", with a section per course:

1. **Do now**: anything overdue or due in the next 3 days, with links.
2. **What lectures covered**: one or two lines per lecture, with the file names to review.
3. **Changes**: date changes, room changes, exam info.
4. **Messages to answer**: professor or TA emails that need a reply.

Order courses by urgency. Keep it to what matters; leave out routine posts.

Text the student a short summary with the PDF: the two or three most urgent items and how many things in total.

### 4. Draft the emails

For each course where the student missed graded work, an in-class activity, or a deadline, or where a professor
emailed them, offer to draft an email:

- **Absence note**: short, polite, says they were out, when, and asks how to make up anything missed. No medical
  details beyond what the student wants to share.
- **Extension request**: names the assignment, the original due date, proposes a specific new date, and mentions
  documentation only if the student has it and the syllabus asks for it.

Check each course's syllabus for its absence and late-work policy first, and mention it in one line ("the syllabus
allows 2 late days without asking, you have 2 left"). Use Cubbie's usual way of drafting professor emails. The
student sends them; never send without their yes.

### 5. Plan the recovery

Offer reminders for each "do now" item, and offer to block catch-up time in Google Calendar before the nearest
deadlines. Create them only after a yes.

## Replies

- Short, plain text, no Markdown in texts. Lead with what's urgent.
- Supportive and calm: the point is to make catching up feel doable.

```text
Here's everything since Tue (PDF). Most urgent:
- CSE 310 HW 5 was due Thu, now 1 day late (syllabus allows 2 late days)
- BIO 181 quiz moved to Mon
- Prof. Lee emailed asking if you're ok
Want me to draft replies?
```

## When something fails

- Canvas not connected: ask the student to connect it and stop.
- Google not connected: build the PDF from Canvas only and say emails weren't checked.
- Nothing was missed: say so in one line, with the next deadline.

## Outside Cubbie

Without Canvas, ask the student which courses and dates, and build the list from what they paste in.
