# Club Radar

Club life runs on email newsletters, last-minute "room change!!" messages and registration links that close
quietly. Students join clubs and then miss the meetings, the sign-up deadline for the trip, or the info session with
free food. Club Radar watches the inbox for all of it, keeps the calendar right, and nudges the student at the
right moment, with a phone call for the things that really matter.

## When to use

- "what club stuff is happening this week?"
- "I joined Robotics Club and the Hackathon Society, keep me posted"
- "remind me to register for the AI club trip"
- "anything with free food today?"
- A forwarded club email or a screenshot of a flyer or group chat
- The daily scan below and Sunday Radar's weekly brief

## Steps

### 1. Learn the student's clubs

The first time, ask which clubs and orgs they're in or curious about, and for each one whether it's
**important** (calls allowed) or **normal** (texts only). Remember the list and the levels, and update them
whenever the student mentions a club. Also remember whether they want to hear about free food.

### 2. Scan for club activity

Look at the student's synced email and Gmail from the last few days (or since the last scan) for:

- Meetings and events: club names from the list, plus words like meeting, GBM, general body, info session, social,
  workshop, tryouts, auditions, rehearsal, volunteer, trip, career fair.
- Registrations and deadlines: RSVP, register, sign up, form closes, dues, applications due, limited spots.
- Changes: moved, rescheduled, cancelled, postponed, new room, new time, "update".
- Free food mentions: pizza, food provided, snacks, lunch, boba, dinner, if the student wants those.

If the student shares their school's public club event page (the campus club platform, a club website), read it with
the web too. Screenshots of flyers and group chats the student sends count the same way.

For each find, note the club, what it is, date and time, place, any link (registration or RSVP), the deadline if
there's one, and the email it came from. Skip newsletters with nothing actionable, and anything already handled.

### 3. Put it on the calendar

- Show new finds in one short text and ask which to add to Google Calendar. Add only the ones the student picks,
  with the location and the registration or RSVP link in the event notes, and "Club Radar" in the description so
  later changes can find it.
- Check each new event against the student's calendar and Canvas deadlines. If it overlaps a class or falls the
  night before an exam or a big deadline, say so in the same text ("Robotics GBM is Wed 7 pm, the night before your
  CSE 310 midterm").
- Ask once whether Club Radar may update events it added when the club changes the time or room. If yes, remember
  it, update those events on its own, and text a one-line heads-up after each change. If no, ask each time.
  Never change or delete events Club Radar didn't add.

### 4. Remind and call

- **Registration deadlines**: a text the day before the form closes and the morning it closes, with the link on its
  own line. Registering is the student's job: Cubbie can open the form in its browser and fill in details the
  student gave, but only submits after the student says yes, and the student handles any payment themselves.
- **Normal clubs**: a text 1 hour before the event (time, room, link).
- **Important clubs**: a text 1 hour before, plus a phone call 20 minutes before, if the student agreed to calls.
  Ask before the first call: "Want a quick call 20 min before Robotics meetings?"
- **Changes**: a text as soon as a change is found, for any event already on the calendar. Move that event's
  reminders and any phone call to the new time (or cancel them if the event is cancelled), so nothing fires at the
  old time.
- Word every reminder so it stands alone later: "Club Radar: Robotics Club GBM at 7 pm today in ENGRC 101. RSVP
  link in the calendar event."

### 5. Daily scan

Offer a daily scan once (default 8 am), worded so it stands alone: "Club Radar daily: scan the student's email for
club meetings, events, registrations and changes since yesterday, and text anything new or changed." Create it only
after a yes. If nothing is new, don't text.

## Replies

- Short, plain text, no Markdown. One event per line: club, what, day and time, place.
- Links on their own line. A pizza emoji for free food is fine.
- At most three events per text; anything longer goes in a short document.

```text
This week:
- Robotics Club GBM, Wed 7 pm, ENGRC 101 🍕
- AI Club trip sign-up closes Thu 11:59 pm
https://forms.example.edu/ai-trip
Add the GBM to your calendar?
```

## When something fails

- Gmail or Calendar not connected: ask the student to connect Google, and offer to work from forwarded emails or
  screenshots in the meantime.
- No club activity found: say so in one line, only when the student asked.
- A date or time is unclear in the email: say what's unclear and ask, don't guess.

## With Radar Autopilot

If the student turned on Radar Autopilot, its scheduled sweeps already run this Skill, so don't offer this Skill's own
recurring check-in. Reminders and calendar entries for new finds are covered by the student's autopilot yes, so create
them without asking again (phone calls only if the student left calls on). Sending, posting, submitting, registering
and paying still need a yes every time.

## Outside Cubbie

Without Google connected, work from emails or flyers the student pastes, and list the dates and times so they can
add them to their own calendar.
